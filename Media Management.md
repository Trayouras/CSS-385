There are ways that media items are used in nearly game, regardless of genre: character models/sprites, textures, sound effects, voices, splash screens, and music are present in nearly every game ever made, besides text-based games (though even these can use them sometimes). Roguelikes, like I am making, don’t often have cutscenes or prominent stories so most don’t have voice acting, and 3D games (at least at the level of complexity I am working at) don’t often have 2D sprites, but the other items are all still applicable. These games need character models, textures to go on models, sound effects and music for a good auditory experience, and splash screen images so menus are usable. 
Overall, Unity has pretty set ways to add media artifacts, but there are some options you have regarding what you add and how exactly you use it. Videos can be rendered as a texture and applied to an object, which can then be used either as a fancy texture on a normal game object or as a full cutscene by focusing the camera on the object. While these two implementations are wildly different, they are both common and are often used together, as working with the camera methods that already exist is much simpler than hijacking the screen altogether. You can also stream videos in to a Unity project, which saves storage space but causes the video to be subject to normal streaming restrictions like internet speed. Models can be spread throughout the game world as either interactable or non-interactable decorations, but if you use both you run the risk of confusing players. If they are aware of objects that can be interacted with in some way, they may think other objects are interactable as well. A notorious example of this is red barrels, as most games that include red barrels give the player some way to cause them to explode, but when a game does not players tend to get upset. 
Beyond whatever I implement for the media assignment (which may well be a button to trigger a popup video texture on a cube) I don’t intend to use any videos in my game, as there’s really no place I can see them being implemented that will make the game better. I also will have it baked into the game instead of streamed in because it will be far more convenient to have the video load with the rest of the game. I originally intended to have some of the stationary game objects be destructible, but I have shifted away from that idea. On top of causing the aforementioned issue of either needing everything to be destructible or making the destructible objects very clear, an endless wave-based game will leave the objects destroyed for most of the game, and at that point they might as well not exist.
